1 · Published post

Between the Chamber and the Window

Duke-UNC Rotary Peace Center · June 22, 2026
Introduction

I wrote this post for the Duke-UNC Rotary Peace Center's Summer 2026 AFE Blog Post Series, and it was published on June 22, 2026. It reflects on my summer at UNICEF's Office of Internal Audit and Investigations and argues that AI cuts both ways for children. It can widen access to learning, but it also fuels harms such as sexualized deepfakes and privacy violations. I wrote it for current and prospective Rotary Peace Fellows and for readers who follow AI governance and child protection. My supervisor, my program director and another Peace Fellow all gave me positive feedback on it.

Blog post on the Rotary Peace Center website
Read the post
Why it matters

This piece sets out the position I bring to AI governance work. Child safety has to be designed into AI systems from the start, not added once the technology already exists. It also asks the question I keep returning to, which is what we owe the people on the other end of the systems we build.

2 · Program design

Peace Lab Project

AI Literacy and Ethics in the Government Schools of Bihar, India
PUBPOL 763 · Fall 2025 · 17 pages
Introduction

I designed and wrote this 17-page program proposal in Fall 2025 for PUBPOL 763, Introduction to Peace and Conflict Resolution. It proposes a community-based AI literacy and ethics intervention for Grades 6 to 9 in government schools in Bihar, India, delivered by teachers and locally hired youth trainers, with a stakeholder analysis, a theory of change and a measurement plan. It earned a High Pass. I later presented it as a poster at Duke and at the Rotary Annual Spring Conference, where many attendees said they would like to see it become a real project.

Proposal (PDF, 17 pages) and conference poster
Open the proposal View the poster
Why it matters

The design assumes low connectivity. Only about 18.5 percent of Bihar's schools have internet access, so activities run offline and in two languages, led by trainers from the community. It also links AI literacy to peacebuilding, drawing on field evidence that classroom teaching can reduce misinformation. It shows how I approach a problem from the last mile inward.

3 · Interactive course

Making Safe Decisions in the Field

Personal project · Articulate Rise 360 · Fall 2026
Introduction

In Fall 2026 I designed and built this scenario-based course for anyone traveling for work. Learners spend their first days in a fictional country and make branching decisions as the security situation changes, then take a graded check at the end. I wrote the content and published the course from Articulate Rise 360 as a web course. It is a recent build, and I do not have usage data for it.

Web course built in Articulate Rise 360
Open the course
Why it matters

The course turns rules into practice. Learners rehearse judgment calls in a safe setting, and a graded check shows whether the judgment holds. I wrote screen reader descriptions so more learners can take it, and a disclaimer states that the setting is fictional and the course is not official guidance. It shows how I design learning for decisions rather than recall.

4 · App prototype

AcademiaConnect

EDUC 761 · Spring 2026
Introduction

I built AcademiaConnect in Spring 2026 for EDUC 761, Design of Emerging Technologies for Education. It is an app prototype that helps PhD students keep their papers in one library, draft with AI suggestions they can accept or decline, and find peers to collaborate with. I tested it with five users, four PhD students and one MEITE student, in think-aloud sessions of 15 to 25 minutes. All five described it as useful and needed for PhD work. They also found gaps, including unclear library labels and a message button that did nothing.

Working prototype and user testing report
Open the prototype Read the testing report
Why it matters

Testing changed the design. I renamed the library sections to Uploaded papers and My notes & Drafts, moved AI suggestions to the top of Writing Mode and enlarged the writing canvas. I treated a request for wellbeing tracking as out of scope for this version. The project shows how I let user evidence set priorities and keep scope in check.

5 · Published article

Towards a Brighter Future: Innovation and AI in the Education Sector

NSDC Connect · Vol. 1, Issue 7 · March 2024 · pp. 21–23
Introduction

This article ran as a Deep Dive feature in the March 2024 issue of NSDC Connect, the magazine of India's National Skill Development Corporation. I wrote it while working at NewGlobe Education. It argues that generative AI can help education teams produce quality content at scale, for example a science textbook in about two months instead of six to eight. It also argues that teams need training in prompt engineering and that fact-checking and original thinking must stay with people. The issue's editorial page lists me under special thanks.

Magazine feature, pp. 21–23 of the issue. The PDF opens on NSDC's site.
Read the article
Why it matters

It marks where my thinking on AI and education stood in early 2024, before my graduate work began. The gap it names is one I still work on, since about a quarter of rural 14 to 18 year olds cannot read a Grade 2 text, yet over 90 percent live in households with a smartphone.
